What Is It

Sterile Filling, Defined

Sterile filling is the process of dosing a liquid or powder drug product into its final container — vial, ampoule, or syringe — under conditions free of microbial contamination.

01

It happens inside a cleanroom, an environment engineered to control air particles, pressure, and humidity.

02

Every surface, operator, and machine component is qualified to meet strict pharmaceutical standards.

How It Works

Dosing With Micron Precision

Automated fillers use calibrated pumps — peristaltic, piston, or time-pressure — to deliver an exact volume into each container.

Vials receive controlled volumes through needle or nozzle dosing heads.

Ampoules are filled and sealed in a single continuous cycle.

Syringes are dosed with sub-milliliter accuracy for injectable therapies.

WHY PRECISION MATTERS

Protecting the Patient, Batch by Batch

An underfilled dose can leave a patient undertreated. An overfilled one can waste active ingredient across an entire production run.

Patient safety: exact dosing ensures the labeled strength matches what's inside the container.

Batch integrity: consistent fills prevent costly rejections and full-batch losses.

Reliable automation on the filling line means every unit meets the same standard — every time.
